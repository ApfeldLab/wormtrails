This imaging setup is highly adaptable to fit the specific imaging needs at hand, and all utilized components may reasonably be substituted. Most critical are a sturdy, opaque enclosure, a sufficiently large diffuse light source, and a camera which can be mounted in a manner which minimizes vibrations. 

Our implementation uses a storage cabinet with all but one shelf removed, a Photomyne film scanning light pad, and a 12 megapixel monochromatic Flir Blackfly S USB3 with a 12mm Basler C-mount lens mounted to the inner top of the cabinet with an inverted Koolertron LCD Digital Microscope Stand. Vibrations were dampened by attaching packing foam pads to the rubber feet of the stand, and spreading the contact area by mounting the height adjustment mechanism upside down inside the cabinet through two holes drilled in the top of the cabinet and into the underside of the base of the stage. This mechanism could likely be simplified by use of a tripod inside the cabinet. 

While not strictly necessary, the 3D printable light guide substantially increases contrast and therefore sensitivity to small brightness changes. Our implementation uses a light guide constructed from cardboard, but has the same structure as the provided `light_guide.scad`. If using the light guide, a glass pane is recommended to support the plates being imaged as opposed to balancing the plates directly on the light guide. Likewise, inter-frame averaging is not required, but improves signal to noise ratio. The degree of improvement will depend on the specific camera used. The provided scripts perform the inter-frame averaging at capture time, and `flir_framesplit_capture.py` saves separate video files for each region.

We recommend writing a custom capture script depending on your specific needs.
